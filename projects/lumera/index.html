Structured evidence extraction for chest radiographs

LUMERA turns a chest radiograph into structured, traceable imaging evidence. It does not produce a diagnosis by itself. It identifies what can be observed from the image, describes the quality and limitations of that observation, and provides anatomically grounded evidence for a separate reasoning layer.

A model should not only state what it predicts. It should state what it can support, what it cannot assess, and why.

How it works

From image to evidence, not to a label

Each stage adds information about the observation and how far it can be trusted. Extraction stays separate from interpretation: LUMERA hands structured evidence to a downstream reasoning system instead of reasoning itself.

Direct prediction
Image→label, score or report
LUMERA direction
Image→structured visual evidence→reliability-aware evidence→reasoning
  1. Chest radiographThe input image
  2. Image and view assessmentIs the image technically adequate, and which view is it?
  3. Anatomy, landmarks, visual structures and measurementsAnatomical masks and landmarks; measurements derived from image geometry
  4. Confidence, limitations and evidence statusAttached to each structure and measurement, not one global score
  5. Structured evidence for downstream reasoningTraceable to image structures and measurements

The problem

A single score hides the questions that matter

Direct prediction systems can be useful, but they often hide:

  • What image region supported the result?
  • Was the relevant anatomy visible?
  • Was the image technically adequate?
  • Was the output based on a reliable measurement?
  • Is the model uncertain because the finding is absent, because the image is poor, or because the system cannot assess it?

What makes LUMERA different

Conventional image modelLUMERA direction
Produces labels or probabilitiesProduces structured evidence objects
Confidence is often a single scoreConfidence is attached to anatomy, measurements and limitations
May hide missing or unsupported informationMakes non-assessability explicit
Often optimised around classification accuracyDesigned around anatomical consistency and evidence validity
Can mix observation and interpretationSeparates extraction from downstream reasoning
Output may be difficult to auditOutput is intended to be traceable to image structures and measurements

LUMERA does not claim conventional models are wrong. It investigates whether an explicit evidence layer can make downstream medical reasoning safer, more inspectable and easier to validate.

Research hypothesis

Evidence-Guided (EG) Hypothesis · not yet proven

EG proposes that a system may become more reliable when it checks not only a model's prediction confidence, but also whether the prediction is supported by consistent anatomical and technical evidence. It is not a validated method or a clinical technique.

Prediction confidence
+ Image-quality evidence
+ Anatomical consistency
+ Measurement validity
+ Cross-checks between evidence sources
→
Reliability-aware structured outputTrusted, limited, or withheld
Confidence alone may not be sufficient to identify unreliable outputs. Anatomical consistency, image quality and evidence agreement may provide additional information about when an extraction should be trusted, limited or withheld.

Cases EG is meant to tell apart

A high-confidence result from an image with inadequate visibility
A plausible segmentation that produces inconsistent measurements
A prediction that conflicts with anatomical geometry
A measurement that cannot be valid because required anatomy is unavailable
An output from an unsupported channel that must not be read as absence

If the hypothesis is validated

Through properly separated datasets and external evaluation, EG could contribute to systems that are:

  • More transparent about what they observed
  • Better at withholding unreliable evidence
  • More useful for downstream reasoning systems
  • Easier to audit than direct end-to-end predictions
  • More robust to poor image quality, cropped anatomy, unusual views and missing structures
  • Better able to separate negative evidence from insufficient evidence

The intended benefit is not a higher benchmark score. It is more trustworthy structured evidence.

What stays private

The exact implementation, routing logic, validation rules, internal thresholds and future experimental design remain private research material. This page describes the hypothesis at a public level only.

Current status

A structured imaging-evidence research platform under active development

Work so far includes:

  • Chest-radiograph preprocessing and view / quality assessment
  • Anatomical masks and landmarks
  • Measurements derived from image geometry
  • Structured findings with explicit validity states
  • A separation between extraction and reasoning
  • Reproducible training, caching, augmentation, checkpoint continuation and benchmark workflows
  • Lung and cardiac-silhouette development benchmarking
  • Reliability comparison between confidence and image-quality proxy signals
It should be described asA structured imaging-evidence research platform under active development.
It should not be presented asA clinical product, medical device, diagnostic service or validated decision-support system.

Development-only results

Future work

What comes next

  1. External validation

    Test on data not used in training, calibration, threshold selection or model selection.

  2. Patient- and site-level separation

    Meaningful separation across patients, institutions, scanners and acquisition settings.

  3. Measurement validation

    Compare anatomical measurements with expert references and evaluate their reliability.

  4. Learned reliability models

    Test whether EG-style evidence signals improve error detection beyond ordinary confidence scores.

  5. View and technical quality

    Evaluate position, projection, rotation, exposure, motion, cropping and artefact handling with real labels.

  6. Broader anatomy support

    Expand supervision for structures that are unavailable or only partly supported today.

  7. Downstream reasoning evaluation

    Test whether LUMERA evidence improves a frozen reasoning system compared with ordinary model outputs.

  8. Human expert evaluation

    Assess whether the output is understandable, useful, auditable and safely limited.
