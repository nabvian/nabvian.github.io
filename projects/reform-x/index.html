Every formulation decision starts with the evidence

RE-FORM X is a proprietary research workspace for pharmaceutical formulation development. It connects evidence collection, formulation design, scientific modelling and laboratory execution, so a research team can keep the basis of each decision, identify promising candidates within defined scientific limits, record uncertainty and prepare the experiments needed for confirmation.

The problem

Formulation evidence is scattered

Formulation research spreads evidence, assumptions, calculations, experimental records and model outputs across papers, spreadsheets and separate laboratory systems. Later, it is hard to say which evidence supported a candidate, whether a model was used inside its validated domain, and what must be tested next.

The approach

One traceable path to a testable decision

RE-FORM X keeps the source, its permissions and a verbatim snapshot; records reviewed observations and the target profile; screens whether the evidence supports the work; generates mass-balanced candidates inside declared constraints; promotes a model only when predefined gates pass independent review; and abstains or rejects instead of guessing. RE-FORM X supports expert scientific work. It does not replace formulation scientists, analytical scientists, quality reviewers or regulatory decisions.

Architecture · four connected stages

From scattered evidence to a laboratory record

Each stage hands the next a recorded, reviewable result, and the link from research decision to experimental plan to laboratory record is preserved.

Stage 1RE-FORM · Evidence
  • Collects product, formulation, scientific and regulatory evidence
  • Stores source permissions, snapshots, observations and review decisions
  • Retains the origin of important assumptions
Stage 2Formulation
  • Defines the intended product profile and formulation constraints
  • Compares excipients, compositions and target dissolution profiles
  • Generates candidates within declared scientific boundaries
Stage 3Science Experiment
  • Records model inputs, assumptions, data splits, metrics and uncertainty
  • Rejects weak models; abstains outside the supported domain
  • Creates confirmation experiment plans instead of treating a prediction as proof
Stage 4Laboratory Execution
  • Tracks material lots, quantities, aliquots, samples, runs, results and instrument files
  • Configurable CSV import while instrument vendors are undecided
  • Links each record back to its decision and plan
Across every stage: authentication, roles and account security · quality investigations · a tamper-evident, hash-chained audit trail · durable background jobs · reproducible report export.

Second demonstration · official clinical-registry data

When the evidence is not enough, it says so

The official aggregate ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02252965 record (API 2.0.5) was retained verbatim with a SHA-256 digest; no patient-level data was used. The source policy allowed retain, extract, display and export, and explicitly not model training.

50aggregate observations extracted, each bound to an exact JSON location in the snapshot
4selected observations independently checked; all others left pending review
2study-arm products registered, with no identity or approval inferred
71events in the run's audit hash chain, verified valid

Decision: INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE

The registry outcomes are real and traceable, but they do not supply the product-specific approval and formulation evidence needed to assert an opportunity. So the screening fails closed: no opportunity score and no success probability are invented, and human review is required.

Missing prerequisites named by the engine:

absorption assessmenthalf-life (hours)modified-release safety assessmentsolubilityapproval for this product and jurisdiction

Revoking the source's current-use permission blocks the imported products and observations, new screenings and the export of earlier reports.

Future upgrades

What comes next

The next steps move RE-FORM X from a demonstrated software workflow toward prospectively validated use.

  1. Independent scientific review
    • Formulation, analytical and regulatory specialists review the data structure and scientific assumptions
    • Target profile design, model selection and validation rules
    • Candidate-generation constraints and the confirmation plan
  2. Prospective laboratory validation
    • A qualified laboratory manufactures and tests selected candidates
    • Predicted vs observed dissolution; batch-to-batch reproducibility
    • Assay, content uniformity, stability, manufacturability, method suitability
  3. Better and broader datasets
    • More formulation records, APIs and dosage forms
    • Consistent analytical method metadata
    • Failed experiments, stability and process data, with licensing records
  4. Instrument integrations
    • Vendor-specific exports or APIs once instruments are chosen
    • HPLC, dissolution tester, UV spectrophotometer, balance
    • Particle-size analyzer, stability chamber, LIMS exports
  5. Regulatory and quality fitness
    • Intended use and claim boundaries
    • Computerized-system validation, data integrity, change control
    • Access-control and security review; audit-trail review procedures

If it succeeds

Who benefits

Formulation scientists

Move from literature and prior data toward candidates with clearer assumptions, constraints and rationale.

Analytical and laboratory teams

A structured handoff: candidate, target profile, materials, quantities, experimental plan and expected result range.

Quality and technical reviewers

See where a conclusion came from, which data and model version produced it, and whether the system abstained or rejected a result.

Small pharma and biotech teams

Begin evidence-led formulation research without first building a large internal data and workflow platform.

Academic collaborators

Contribute methods, datasets, experiments and scientific critique while preserving a defined evidence trail.

Patients and health systems

Indirectly: better research decisions may reduce wasted experiments, shorten investigation cycles and improve the formulations taken into proper laboratory and clinical development.
