A verdict about one protein variant, and the evidence that produced it.

Name a protein and a single amino-acid substitution in it. PROTEORA assembles what public science has actually measured and curated about that exact change, answers seven separate questions about it, and attaches to every answer the rows it used. Where the held evidence cannot decide a question, it returns a refusal that names what is missing — never a confident-looking number standing in for an absent measurement.

The problem

A single number cannot carry its own doubt

Ask the field what a protein substitution does and the usual answer is one number from a trained model — a score, a probability, a predicted change in stability or binding. Such models are genuinely useful, and they have a shared property: the output looks the same whether it rests on decades of measurement or on nothing at all. The confidence is in the format, not in the evidence.

For a well-studied variant that is a small problem. For the rare and the newly observed — exactly where a researcher most needs help — it is the whole problem. A plausible number where no measurement exists is not a weak answer. It is a wrong one, and it is indistinguishable from a right one.

What I am building

A system whose answers and refusals are equally informative

PROTEORA is an attempt at the other design. It holds no opinion of its own. It reads what sources have published, keeps each measurement as a row that remembers where it came from, and lets a question be decided only by rows that genuinely bear on it.

  • Every claim traces back to the record, publication and row it came from.
  • A refusal states its cause — which source was silent, which was unreachable, which evidence would settle it.
  • Nothing is fused into a score. There is no single number to over-read.
  • Assumptions are printed beside the verdicts that used them, including the ones that are judgement rather than published fact.

How it works

One substitution in; seven answered or refused questions out

The input is deliberately small: a protein identifier and a substitution written the way people write it, such as A123V. Everything else is assembled from public curated science — sequence and structure records, variant archives, measured small-molecule affinities, curated disease links. No private data, no uploads, no patient information at any point.

Gather

Public records for that protein are fetched, completed to the end of their listings, and checked against what each source says it holds.

Store as evidence

Each measurement becomes a typed row carrying its origin, its conditions and the exact form of the protein measured. One measurement restated by several sources stays one row.

Ask

Each question gathers only the rows that bear on it, and decides by a written rule. Rows that are not comparable are not compared.

Report

A verdict per question, each with its rows, its assumptions, and — for a refusal — what is missing and what would settle it.

The four answers it is allowed to give

SUPPORTED

Held rows support the statement, under a rule written down in advance.

CONTRADICTED

Held rows contradict it. Reported as readily as support.

INSUFFICIENT

Evidence is held but cannot decide. The reason says why, and what would.

NOT ASSESSED

The question does not apply, or no source that could answer it covers this protein.

There is deliberately no fifth answer meaning "probably". A probability would be the single number this project exists to avoid.

The seven questions

Each is answered separately, because the evidence for one says nothing about another.

  • Sequence — is this really the reference residue, and do curators record this change?
  • Structure — what covers this position, by annotation and by solved structure?
  • Population — has the change been observed in population sequencing?
  • Clinical — how do clinical archives classify it, and for which condition?
  • Binding — does measured small-molecule affinity change, compound by compound?
  • Interaction — is a change in protein–protein binding measured?
  • Pathway and disease — what is linked to the gene, as distinct from this variant?

Four rules that never bend

  • Unknown is refused and counted, never quietly rendered as "no effect".
  • Unlike measurements are never merged — different kinds of experiment stay in separate channels even when the numbers look comparable.
  • One experiment is one witness, however many places restate it.
  • Evidence about one species is never read onto another's protein, even where the gene name matches.

Each of these was added after it caught a real error in this system's own output. Several are described under Status below.

The research

Four hypotheses, named but not yet proven

PROTEORA exists to test four connected propositions. They are stated here as claims that could fail, because each one can. Their internals are not published; what follows is what each is for and how it would be judged.

RKMS

Evidence can be stored so that its origin never detaches from it

A store in which every claim keeps its lineage, its conditions and the exact form of the entity measured — and in which the same measurement arriving from several directions is recognised as one, not counted repeatedly.

Fails if provenance cannot survive storage and retrieval without being rebuilt, or if restatements cannot be told from independent results.

CASCADE

A question can be compiled into exactly the evidence that bears on it

Rather than searching a general structure of everything known, each question generates the narrow relation it needs, decides by a written rule, and returns a verdict with its rows. Conflicting evidence is reconciled in the open, without collapsing into a score.

Fails if questions cannot be answered without a persistent general structure, or if reconciliation cannot avoid a hidden weighting.

ESF

A query needs only a small, local state — not the whole knowledge base

The working state for one question is assembled for that question and discarded after it, so answering stays bounded no matter how much evidence exists in total.

Fails if the state needed grows with the size of the store rather than with the question.

COMPUTING

Computation should be spent on what would change the answer

When evidence cannot decide, the system already knows what is missing. That same statement should direct which calculation or experiment to run next — effort aimed at the specific doubt, rather than at producing a number for its own sake.

Status: design only. Specified and reviewed; deliberately not built until the three above are standing.

The target the whole design is judged against: no unsupported claim, and no dropped claim. Nothing asserted that the evidence does not carry, and nothing the evidence does carry silently lost. Both failures are errors; only the first is usually counted.

The refusals outnumber the claims by fourteen to one, and most are a single question asked separately of several hundred compounds. Each carries its own reason.

What a claim looks like

Four of the seventy-two, in the system's own words:

  • EGFR L858R — classified with a strong clinical-impact tier, and separately as a drug-response variant on a record reviewed by an expert panel.
  • BRAF V600E — 228 structure chains across 123 solved entries cover residue 600; the reference residue there is V.
  • BRAF K601E — classified pathogenic, oncogenic, and at a potential clinical-impact tier, each by a named archive record.
  • EGFR C797S — five claims only. The records hold far less about it, and the report says so rather than padding.

The contradiction, and why it is right

BRAF V600E is CONTRADICTED as a germline-pathogenic variant while simultaneously SUPPORTED as oncogenic. That is not a conflict in the system; it is the biology. The archive's germline classifications for it conflict, and it is a somatic driver, not an inherited pathogenic allele.

A system that reported one number for this variant would have to choose. This one reports both, each against the record it came from.

A claim this exercise withdrew

Enumerating these claims turned up a wrong one. EGFR T790M was supported as binding a compound far more weakly, "in 2 independent publications". Checking those two documents showed one was a paper and the other a data deposition re-stating that same paper's numbers — one experiment, counted twice. The rule that detects restatement was too narrow: it required the two records to carry identical sets of values, and the deposition carried a subset.

The rule was widened, the benchmark re-registered and re-run to confirm nothing else moved, and that verdict is now a refusal naming the restatement. It is the third time one experiment has passed as two witnesses, each time escaping the previous fix by a narrower margin. Public databases restate measurements in forms that closely resemble independent corroboration, and each route has to be found and closed on its own.

It was found by reading two proteins by hand. The benchmark, which could answer only ten units, could never have reached it — which is the argument for doing both.

What this demonstrates, and what it does not. It shows the system runs end to end and produces traceable, checkable claims on real public evidence. It is not a measurement of accuracy: two proteins chosen for being well studied cannot establish how the system behaves on a protein nobody has studied, and the claims above were not scored against an independent reference. That remains the open problem.

Boundaries

What PROTEORA is not, and will not become

  • Not a medical device, and not clinical software. It gives no diagnosis, no prognosis and no treatment guidance, for any person.
  • Not a predictor. It makes no claim about variants nobody has measured. Where there is no evidence, the answer is a refusal, not an estimate.
  • Not a scoring system. There is no overall number, no ranking and no confidence percentage to quote out of context.
  • Not a literature reader. It takes figures from curated records, never by interpreting the text of a paper.
  • Not a source of new measurements. Everything it reports was measured by someone else and is attributed to them.
  • Not validated. No part of it has been through clinical, regulatory or independent scientific validation.

Where it stands — version 0.1, frozen 15 October 2026

Runs end to end; its central claim remains untested

Every figure on this page is taken from version 0.1, which is now frozen. It will not drift under the numbers quoted here: a benchmark record only means something while the thing it measured stays still. Work on the next version happens in a separate copy, and anything that changes these figures will be published as its own version with its own measurements.

The zero, and why it is the point

On the binding question, against that public benchmark, PROTEORA makes zero supported claims. Standing alone that number is ambiguous: it fits a system with nothing to say, and equally a system quietly losing the evidence it holds.

So the refusals were audited. A checker written separately, in another language, reading the raw source records rather than anything the system produced, asked of all 441 refusals whether the records in fact held a comparable pair of measurements. All 441 were confirmed warranted; none hid a measurement. Run against the ten units the system did answer, as a control, the same checker found the pair every time — so the zeros are informative rather than a broken instrument.

The cause is ordinary and traceable: for 342 of those units the compound was never measured against that protein at all, and for most of the rest never in a comparable way. The benchmark and the available measurements barely overlap.

That is a finding about the state of public data as much as about this system. A separate test, below, asked whether a different benchmark would help, and measured that it would not.

What the testing did establish

  • It withdrew its own claim. The one supported verdict it produced turned out to rest on a single experiment recorded twice. The rule was tightened and the claim retracted.
  • It caught a cross-species error. Evidence about a human gene was being read onto a non-human protein of the same name. Now structurally impossible.
  • Every run was pre-registered. Inputs fixed and fingerprinted before each run; one earlier run was declared void rather than reported, because its setup had drifted.
  • Failures stay in the record. Including the void run and the withdrawn claim.

Why accuracy cannot yet be measured, and why that is itself a result

Checking whether a supported verdict is right needs an independent answer to check it against: a second laboratory, in a paper this system never read, measuring both the changed and the unchanged protein against the same compound. The test was built that way and registered before the proteins were fetched, and the evidence base was widened from 56 proteins to 71 to give it the best chance of succeeding.

Across those 71 proteins the system makes 18 supported claims about binding. For 17 of them no independent measurement exists. For nine, other laboratories measured the compound but only ever against one form of the protein. For eight, nobody else measured it at all. The public benchmark cannot help either: none of the cases it labels is one this system can decide, so no amount of re-running it would have answered the question.

The single case that could be checked points the same way. For a well-known drug-resistance change, the system reports binding 25 to 45 times weaker, and an independent laboratory measured 19 times weaker — the same direction, just short of the threshold the rules require before counting it. One agreeing case proves nothing, and is not offered as proof.

The obstacle is the published record, not this benchmark or this system. A second laboratory independently measuring both forms, and publishing it separately, is close to absent from the public databases; nearly everything decidable here traces back to a handful of large screening panels. That bounds what any system of this kind can presently be shown to do, and building the data to lift that bound is the honest next piece of work.

A limitation found after this page was first published

Checking the structural question across every protein held, not only the two worked through above, found 44 of 227 supported structural verdicts resting on a predicted annotation and nothing else — no curated or experimentally observed annotation covering that residue.

The statement each of those verdicts makes is literally true: an annotation does cover the residue, and a predicted annotation is an annotation. But the verdict is displayed as supported without saying that prediction is all that supports it, and a reader may take it for a settled fact about the protein. By this project's own rule a prediction should not carry a claim on its own.

None of the 72 claims in the worked example above is affected — every one of them rests on at least one curated or observed record, and that was checked rather than assumed. The fix is to carry the evidence class on the verdict itself, as is already done where a clinical record rests on a single submitter. It is recorded here before it is made, because a limitation found after publishing is still a limitation.

If the hypotheses hold

What it could offer, stated conditionally because it is conditional

None of the following is claimed today. Each follows only if the hypotheses above survive testing that has not yet happened.

An honest map of what is known

For any variant, a clear separation between what has actually been measured, what has been measured but cannot settle the question, and what nobody has looked at — each distinguishable at a glance.

Experiments chosen by what is missing

A system that records precisely why it could not decide can propose the specific measurement that would decide it. Scarce laboratory effort could follow the doubt instead of the convention.

Evidence that survives review

Conclusions that can be re-derived from their sources months later, by someone else, without rerunning a model or trusting the person who produced them.

A measurable floor under predictions

Predictive models could be scored against what is genuinely known versus genuinely unknown, rather than against mixtures of the two.

Reasoning that does not collapse at scale

If a query's cost tracks the question rather than the size of the knowledge base, the same approach holds as evidence accumulates.

A pattern beyond proteins

Nothing in the four hypotheses is specific to molecular biology. Any field where conclusions must survive audit faces the same problem.

How it grows

The order of work, and what has to be true at each step

It grows by addition, not by redesign

The three layers underneath — the evidence store, the question compiler and the query-local working state — are the part that has been measured and that works. Nothing planned replaces them. New capability arrives above them: further areas of science as new question types, and a controller that decides which evidence is worth gathering next. Both read what is already there and write ordinary evidence rows.

This is a design constraint rather than an aspiration, and it is checked the same way anything else here is. Four properties must survive every addition, because they are the results rather than decoration: the working state tracks the question, not the size of the database; no stored web of relationships is traversed to answer anything; four verdicts and no fused score; and every refusal carries its reason. An addition that costs one of them has failed, whatever else it offers.

A consequence worth stating plainly: a controller deciding what to compute may use a probability internally to choose its next step, and that probability still never becomes the answer. The reported output stays the four verdicts and the rows behind them.

    • Records gathered and completed; seven questions answered with their rows; every run reproducible
  2. Adversarial self-testingDone
    • Pre-registered benchmark runs; four defects found and fixed; one run voided and one claim withdrawn in the record
  3. Data that can judge accuracy at allCurrent
    • Measured, not assumed: of 18 supported claims across 71 proteins, 17 have no independent measurement anywhere in public data
    • Needs paired replication — a second laboratory measuring both forms of the same protein against the same compound
    • Until that exists, accuracy here is untestable for any system, and saying so is part of the work
  4. Independent review
    • Verdicts checked by people who did not build the system, on proteins they choose
    • Disagreements published alongside agreements
  5. Directed computation
    • The fourth hypothesis built, once the first three stand: the recorded gap chooses the next calculation
  6. Wider evidence, same discipline
    • More evidence types, admitted only where provenance and comparability survive
